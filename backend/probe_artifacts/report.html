Reading the two charts together

DDM alone and Sine alone each nudge future‑volatility R² from a ~0.09 input‑only floor to ~0.11–0.12, flat across depth, and their layer‑3 representations stay almost identical to each other (CKA 0.995). DDM+Sine's R² climbs to 0.48 by layer 3, and at that same layer its representation pulls away from both ingredients (CKA 0.854 vs DDM‑only, 0.834 vs Sine‑only) — not a blend of two known representations, but a third one the interaction forms on its own.

Four findings

01

The transfer is specific to volatility dynamics, not a general representation upgrade. Future‑return, kurtosis and local‑structure probes stay within noise (|R²|<0.02) for every checkpoint, including DDM+Sine — the one condition that dominates on volatility.

02

The effect is deep, not shallow. DDM+Sine's volatility R² is barely ahead of the pack at layer 0 (0.125) and only pulls away from layer 2 onward (0.394 → 0.483) — the feature is built by the back half of a 4‑layer network, not carried in from the input.

03

It generalizes to Delay, the other component that showed a dramatic fine‑tuning effect: DDM+Delay traces the same late‑emerging shape (0.124→0.136→0.203→0.386) and the same layer‑3 CKA divergence from DDM‑only (0.907). DDM+XOR — the no‑effect component — shows neither: flat probe curve, CKA 0.988, indistinguishable from DDM alone.

04

DDM alone and Sine alone aren't just similar to a "generic" baseline — they're nearly identical to each other at every layer (CKA ≥ 0.995 throughout). Whatever each contributes on its own barely moves the representation; only together do they build something neither has.
